# Derivation of the cable equation

To model a cell as a one-dimensional cable, we assume that there is a potential gradient along its length ($x$). We will ignore any radial components of the current.

We define the following notation:

- $V_i$ and $V_e$: the intra- and extracellular potentials.
- $I_i$ and $I_e$: the intra- and extracellular axial currents flowing down the length of the cable.
- $r_i$ and $r_e$: the intra- and extracellular resistance per unit length.

The intracellular resistance per unit length is defined by the resistivity of the cytoplasm ($R_c$) and the cross-sectional area of the cable ($A_i$):

```{math}
:label: eq:resistance_per_length
r_i = \frac{R_c}{A_i}
```

## Currents are assumed to be Ohmic

The axial current flowing along the inside of the axon is proportional to the potential difference between two neighbouring points. For a small segment of length $\Delta x$, we have:

```{math}
:label: eq:ohmic_intracellular
V_i(x+\Delta x) - V_i(x) = - I_i(x) r_i \Delta x
```

```{math}
:label: eq:ohmic_extracellular
V_e(x+\Delta x) - V_e(x) = - I_e(x) r_e \Delta x
```

Taking the limit as $\Delta x \to 0$, we find that the axial current is proportional to the spatial derivative of the voltage:

```{math}
:label: eq:axial_current_i
I_i = - \frac{1}{r_i} \frac{\partial V_i}{\partial x}
```

```{math}
:label: eq:axial_current_e
I_e = - \frac{1}{r_e} \frac{\partial V_e}{\partial x}
```

## Conservation of current

Any current leaving the intracellular domain must cross the membrane and enter the extracellular domain, and vice versa. Therefore, total current must be conserved between $x$ and $x+\Delta x$:

```{math}
:label: eq:current_conservation
I_i(x) - I_i(x+\Delta x) = -(I_e(x) - I_e(x+\Delta x)) = I_t \Delta x
```

where $I_t$ is the total transmembrane current per unit length. Taking the limit $\Delta x \to 0$ yields:

```{math}
:label: eq:transmembrane_from_axial
I_t = - \frac{\partial I_i}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial I_e}{\partial x}
```

Inserting our previous Ohmic expressions for the currents gives us the transmembrane current in terms of the second spatial derivative of the voltage:

```{math}
:label: eq:transmembrane_laplacian
I_t = \frac{1}{r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V_i}{\partial x^2} = -\frac{1}{r_e} \frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2}
```

## Relating it to the membrane potential

We now have the membrane current $I_t$ expressed in terms of $V_i$ and $V_e$. However, what we really want is an equation for the total transmembrane potential $V$ (where $V = V_i - V_e$).

Using the relation from above:

```{math}
:label: eq:ve_substitution
\frac{1}{r_e} \frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{1}{r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V_i}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{1}{r_i} \left(\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2}\right)
```

Rearranging the terms:

```{math}
:label: eq:ve_rearranged
\left(\frac{1}{r_e} + \frac{1}{r_i}\right) \frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{1}{r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
```

```{math}
:label: eq:ve_solved
\frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{r_e}{r_e+r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
```

Finally, substituting this back into our equation for $I_t$:

```{math}
:label: eq:it_final
I_t = - \frac{1}{r_e} \frac{\partial^2 V_e}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{r_e+r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
```

## Incorporating cell membrane physiology

We have now shown that the current crossing the membrane at any specific point depends on the spatial curvature (the second derivative) of the voltage around it. But what exactly constitutes that transmembrane current $I_t$?

From our previous cell models, we know that the current crossing the membrane consists of the capacitive current and the ionic currents (the ion channels). If $p$ is the physical circumference of the cable, we can write:

```{math}
:label: eq:membrane_current
I_t = p \left(C_{\mathrm{m}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + I_{\mathrm{ion}}\right)
```

Setting these two definitions of $I_t$ equal to each other gives us the complete 1D biological cable equation:

```{math}
:label: eq:cable_equation
p \left(C_{\mathrm{m}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + I_{\mathrm{ion}}(V)\right) = \frac{1}{r_e+r_i} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
```

Notice how this equation links the change in voltage over time ($\partial V / \partial t$) directly to how the voltage changes over space ($\partial^2 V / \partial x^2$).

## Physical units

It is worth pausing to consider the physical units of the terms in the cable equation, as this provides a useful sanity check. Typical units used in neurophysiology are:

| Quantity | Description | Typical unit |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| $p$ | Cable circumference | cm |
| $C_{\mathrm{m}}$ | Membrane capacitance per unit area | µF/cm² |
| $V$ | Voltage | mV |
| $I_{\mathrm{ion}}$ | Ionic current per unit area | µA/cm² |
| $r_i, r_e$ | Axial resistance per unit length | kΩ/cm |
| $x$ | Position along the cable | cm |
| $t$ | Time | ms |

You may wish to verify that both sides of equation {eq}`eq:cable_equation` have the same physical dimensions. This is a good exercise in dimensional analysis (see {doc}`Exercise 15.1 <../exercises/exercise_15.1_pen_and_paper>`).
