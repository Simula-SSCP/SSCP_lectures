# Electrodiffusion and transmembrane currents

We have established that the cell membrane acts as a capacitor, and that opening ion channels allows charged particles to cross this barrier, generating a current that shifts the membrane potential.

But what actually drives these ions to move in the first place? And how do we determine the magnitude and direction of the resulting electrical current?

In this chapter, we will introduce the concept of **electrodiffusion**—the combined physical forces of chemical diffusion and electrical drift. Using the **Nernst-Planck equation**, we will derive the fundamental **Nernst potential**, which represents the exact electrical balancing point for any given ion. Finally, we will use this potential to define mathematical expressions for transmembrane currents, allowing us to build our very first ordinary differential equation (ODE) model of the cell membrane.
