# Introduction to membrane biophysics

In the previous module, we established the fundamental physical laws governing molecular interactions. We used the law of mass action to track chemical kinetics, explored how ligands bind to proteins, and grounded our models in the strict thermodynamic constraints of Gibbs free energy and diffusion.

Now, we are ready to take those abstract physical chemistry concepts and apply them directly to the living cell.

In this module, we transition into the realm of **electrophysiology**—the study of the electrical properties of biological cells. Our primary goal is to understand how a biological cell, which is essentially just a bag of saltwater, can act as a sophisticated electrical device capable of firing the precise electrical signals that drive every heartbeat and thought in your body.

:::{admonition} To do this, we will explore:
:class: tip

1. **The cell membrane:** How the physical structure of the lipid bilayer forces it to act as an electrical capacitor.
2. **Mass transport and electrodiffusion:** How the principles of diffusion and electrical drift combine to drive charged ions across the membrane through specialized ion channels.
3. **The Nernst potential:** How to mathematically determine the exact equilibrium point where chemical and electrical forces perfectly balance.
   :::

By the end of this module, we will combine these components to derive our very first ordinary differential equation (ODE) describing how the cell's membrane potential changes over time. This foundational model will set the stage for everything we do next: building a complete, dynamic simulation of the action potential.
