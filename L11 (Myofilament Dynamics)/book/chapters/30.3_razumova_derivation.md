# Derivation of cooperative rate constants

This section presents the thermodynamic derivation of the nearest-neighbour cooperativity parameters in the Razumova (2000) model. For the high-level summary and final formulas, refer to {ref}`the main Razumova theory page <sec_razumova_basic>`.

## Nearest-neighbour configurations

Consider a single regulatory unit (RU) that can be in either the *off* (state 1) or the *on* (state 2) configuration. This RU has two nearest neighbours, each of which is independently in state 1 or 2. This gives rise to four possible neighbour configurations:

```{figure} ../../fig/Razumova_Fig2.png
---
width: 400px
name: fig_razumova_fig2
---
All possible nearest-neighbour configurations: both neighbours off (1-1), one on and one off (1-2 and 2-1), and both on (2-2). *(Figure from Razumova et al., 2000.)*
```

## Boltzmann statistics

The transition rates between the on and off states obey Boltzmann statistics:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}}^{*} = k_a\, e^{-B_{12}^{\#}/(\kappa T)}, \qquad k_{\mathrm{off}}^{*} = k_b\, e^{-B_{21}^{\#}/(\kappa T)}
$$

where $k_a$ and $k_b$ are attempt frequencies, $B_{12}^{\#}$ and $B_{21}^{\#}$ are the activation energies for the off→on and on→off transitions respectively, the superscript $\#$ denotes a specific nearest-neighbour configuration, $\kappa$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the absolute temperature.

The exponential term $e^{-B_{ij}^{\#}/(\kappa T)}$ represents the probability that an attempt to make a transition will succeed. A higher activation energy $B_{ij}^{\#}$ means a lower probability of success.

**Physical intuition:** For a RU in the off state, the highest activation energy (hardest transition) occurs when both neighbours are also off (configuration 1-1), and the lowest (easiest) when both neighbours are on (configuration 2-2).

## Averaging over the population

For the entire population of RUs, the effective $k_{\mathrm{on}}$ is obtained by averaging over all neighbour configurations:

```{figure} ../../fig/Neighbor_rates_Razumova.png
---
width: 400px
name: fig_neighbor_rates
---
The population-averaged transition rate as a sum over all nearest-neighbour configurations. *(Figure from Razumova et al., 2000.)*
```

Assuming a random distribution, the likelihood that a neighbour is off is $\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}$ and that it is on is $\lambda^{\mathrm{on}}$:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = k_a \left(\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}\lambda^{\mathrm{off}} e^{-B_{12}^{11}/(\kappa T)} + \lambda^{\mathrm{off}}\lambda^{\mathrm{on}} e^{-B_{12}^{12}/(\kappa T)} + \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}\lambda^{\mathrm{off}} e^{-B_{12}^{21}/(\kappa T)} + \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}\lambda^{\mathrm{on}} e^{-B_{12}^{22}/(\kappa T)}\right)
$$

By symmetry ($B_{12}^{12} = B_{12}^{21}$), this simplifies to:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = k_a\, e^{-B_{12}^{11}/(\kappa T)} \left[ (\lambda^{\mathrm{off}})^2 + 2\lambda^{\mathrm{on}}\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}\, e^{(B_{12}^{11}-B_{12}^{12})/(\kappa T)} + (\lambda^{\mathrm{on}})^2\, e^{(B_{12}^{11}-B_{12}^{22})/(\kappa T)} \right]
$$

## Introducing the cooperativity parameter $u$

Razumova et al. assumed that the interaction between adjacent RUs leads to activation energies satisfying:

$$
B_{12}^{12} - B_{12}^{11} = -U, \qquad B_{12}^{22} - B_{12}^{11} = -2U
$$

where $U > 0$ is the energy reduction per active neighbour. Substituting:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = k_a\, e^{-B_{12}^{11}/(\kappa T)} \left[ (\lambda^{\mathrm{off}})^2 + 2\lambda^{\mathrm{on}}\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}\, e^{U/(\kappa T)} + (\lambda^{\mathrm{on}})^2\, e^{2U/(\kappa T)} \right]
$$

Recognising the perfect square:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = \underbrace{k_a\, e^{-B_{12}^{11}/(\kappa T)}}_{k_{\mathrm{on}}^u} \left[ \lambda^{\mathrm{off}} + \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}\, e^{U/(\kappa T)} \right]^2
$$

Defining $u = e^{U/(\kappa T)}$ and using $\lambda^{\mathrm{off}} = 1 - \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}$, we arrive at:

$$
\boxed{k_{\mathrm{on}}^w = k_{\mathrm{on}}^u \left[1 + \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}(u-1)\right]^2}
$$

The superscript $w$ indicates that this rate will be further modified by XB-RU cooperativity.

A similar derivation for the reverse direction gives:

$$
\boxed{k_{\mathrm{off}}^w = k_{\mathrm{off}}^u \left[u - \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}(u-1)\right]^2}
$$

When $u = 1$ (i.e., $U = 0$, no interaction energy), both expressions reduce to the base rates $k_{\mathrm{on}}^u$ and $k_{\mathrm{off}}^u$, confirming that no cooperativity is present.

## XB-XB and XB-RU cooperativity

The same nearest-neighbour Boltzmann framework is applied to derive the XB-XB coupling (parameter $v$) and the XB-RU coupling (parameter $w$). The key difference is that the relevant state fractions change:

- For **XB-XB cooperativity**, the relevant fraction is $\lambda^{A_2}$ (strongly bound crossbridges):

```{figure} ../../fig/xbxb_Rates.png
---
width: 400px
name: fig_xbxb_rates
---
XB-XB cooperativity rates. *(Figure from Razumova et al., 2000.)*
```

$$
f = f_0\left[1+\lambda^{A_2}(e^{v-1}-1)\right]^2, \qquad f' = f_0'\left[1+\lambda^{A_2}(e^{-(v-1)}-1)\right]^2
$$

- For **XB-RU cooperativity**, the $k_{\mathrm{on}}^w$ and $k_{\mathrm{off}}^w$ rates are further scaled by the fraction of strongly bound XBs:

```{figure} ../../fig/xbru_Rates.png
---
width: 400px
name: fig_xbru_rates
---
XB-RU cooperativity rates. *(Figure from Razumova et al., 2000.)*
```

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = k_{\mathrm{on}}^w\left[1+\lambda^{A_2}(e^{w-1}-1)\right]^2, \qquad k_{\mathrm{off}} = k_{\mathrm{off}}^w\left[1+\lambda^{A_2}(e^{-(w-1)}-1)\right]^2
$$

In both cases, increasing the cooperativity parameter ($v$ or $w$) enhances the forward rate while suppressing the reverse rate, producing a positively reinforcing feedback loop that steepens the macroscopic force-calcium relationship.
