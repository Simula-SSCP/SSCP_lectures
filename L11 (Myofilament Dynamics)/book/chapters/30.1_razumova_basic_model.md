# The stiffness-distortion crossbridge model

The stiffness-distortion model proposed by Razumova, Bukatina, and Campbell (2000) represents a half-sarcomere. Instead of tracking the exact spatial displacement $x$ of every crossbridge (XB) like Huxley did, this model groups the contractile machinery into distinct functional states.

The force generated by the sarcomere is the product of the stiffness of all parallel crossbridges and their average distortion:

$$
F(t) = e \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i(t)\, x_i(t)
$$

where $e$ is the stiffness of a single XB, $A_i(t)$ is the number of XBs in the $i$-th force-generating state, and $x_i(t)$ is the average distortion of XBs in that state.

Both $A_i$ and $x_i$ vary in time and depend on a number of important mechanisms such as calcium availability, filament overlap, regulatory protein dynamics, and XB kinetics. In this model, the authors focused on the latter two mechanisms and held filament overlap and $[\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}]$ constant.

## The four states

The model tracks the proportion of regulatory units (RUs) in four specific states:

- $R_{\mathrm{off}}$: The thin filament is inactive. Myosin cannot bind.
- $D$: The detached state. The thin filament is active, but the XB has not attached yet.
- $A_1$: Attached XB, pre-powerstroke (generating no force).
- $A_2$: Attached XB, post-powerstroke (generating force).

## The governing ODEs

Using inspection and our knowledge of mass-action kinetics, we can write the ODEs governing the transitions between these states:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}D}{\mathrm{d}t} = k_{\mathrm{on}} R_{\mathrm{off}} + f' A_1 + g A_2 - (k_{\mathrm{off}} + f) D
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}A_1}{\mathrm{d}t} = f D + h' A_2 - (f' + h) A_1
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}A_2}{\mathrm{d}t} = h A_1 - (h' + g) A_2
$$

Because the total number of binding sites ($R_T$) must be conserved, we can always find the final state algebraically:

$$
R_{\mathrm{off}} = R_T - D - A_1 - A_2
$$

where $R_T$ represents the total number of crossbridges for a particular filament overlap fraction. Although the model does not explicitly consider filament overlap, one could introduce length-dependence by adjusting $R_T$.

## Force and the rate of tension development

For this simplified version of the model, we consider that the total force produced by the system is proportional to the number of XBs in the post-powerstroke $A_2$ state. If we wish to quantify how quickly the muscle develops tension, we can calculate the **rate of tension development** $k_{\mathrm{dev}}$ by taking the reciprocal of the time required to reach $(1 - 1/e) \cdot F_{\max}$:

$$
k_{\mathrm{dev}} = \frac{1}{t_{63\%}}
$$

where $t_{63\%}$ is the time at which the force first reaches $63\%$ of its steady-state maximum. This quantity is directly comparable to experimental measurements of tension development rate.
