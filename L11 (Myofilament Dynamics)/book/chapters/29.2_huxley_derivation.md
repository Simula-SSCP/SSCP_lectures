# Derivation of the Huxley steady-state solution

This section provides the complete, step-by-step derivation of the analytical steady-state solution $n(x)$ for the Huxley (1957) model. For a high-level overview of the model assumptions and final results, refer to {ref}`the main theory page <sec_huxley_theory>`.

## The governing ODE

At steady state, the crossbridge probability distribution satisfies:

$$
-v\frac{\mathrm{d}n(x)}{\mathrm{d}x} = f(x)[1-n(x)] - g(x)\,n(x)
$$

where $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the piecewise-defined attachment and detachment rates. We solve this equation in each of the three spatial regions independently, then enforce continuity at the boundaries.

## Region 1: $x < 0$

In this region, $f(x)=0$ and $g(x)=g_2$. The ODE simplifies to:

$$
-v\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}x} = -g_2\,n
$$

This is a separable first-order equation. Separating variables and integrating:

$$
\int \frac{\mathrm{d}n}{n} = \int \frac{g_2}{v}\,\mathrm{d}x
$$

$$
\ln n = \frac{g_2}{v}\,x + C_0
$$

Exponentiating both sides yields the general solution for Region 1:

$$
n(x) = C_1\, e^{g_2 x / v}
$$

where $C_1 = e^{C_0}$ is a constant to be determined from boundary conditions.

## Region 2: $0 < x < h$

Here, $f(x) = f_1 x/h$ and $g(x) = g_1 x/h$. The ODE becomes:

$$
-v\,h\,\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}x} = f_1 x(1-n) - g_1 x\, n
$$

Rearranging:

$$
-v\,h\,\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}x} = x(f_1+g_1)\left(\frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1} - n\right)
$$

This is again separable. Letting $\alpha = f_1/(f_1+g_1)$:

$$
\int \frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\alpha - n} = \int \frac{f_1+g_1}{v\,h}\,x\,\mathrm{d}x
$$

$$
-\ln(\alpha - n) = \frac{f_1+g_1}{v\,h}\,\frac{x^2}{2} + C_2
$$

$$
n(x) = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1} - C_3\,e^{\frac{(f_1+g_1)x^2}{2vh}}
$$

where $C_3 = e^{C_2}$.

## Region 3: $x > h$

No crossbridges can be attached beyond $x=h$:

$$
n(x) = 0 \quad \text{for } x > h
$$

## Determining the constants

We apply the **continuity conditions** at $x=h$ and $x=0$:

**At $x = h$:** $n(h^-) = n(h^+) = 0$, so:

$$
0 = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1} - C_3\,e^{\frac{(f_1+g_1)h^2}{2vh}}
$$

$$
C_3 = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\,e^{-\frac{(f_1+g_1)h^2}{2vh}}
$$

Substituting back:

$$
n(x) = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\left(1 - e^{-\frac{(f_1+g_1)}{2vh}(h^2-x^2)}\right) \quad \text{for } 0 \le x \le h
$$

**At $x = 0$:** $n(0^-) = n(0^+)$, so:

$$
C_1 = n(0^+) = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\left(1 - e^{-\frac{(f_1+g_1)h^2}{2vh}}\right)
$$

giving:

$$
n(x) = \frac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\left(1 - e^{-\frac{(f_1+g_1)h^2}{2vh}}\right)e^{g_2 x/v} \quad \text{for } x < 0
$$

## Compact form

Defining $\phi = \frac{h}{S}(f_1+g_1)$ and $V_{\mathrm{norm}} = \frac{2v}{S}$ (where $S$ is the sarcomere length), the three regions simplify to:

$$
\begin{cases}
x<0: & n(x) = \dfrac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\left(1-e^{-\phi/V_{\mathrm{norm}}}\right)e^{2g_2 x/(S V_{\mathrm{norm}})} \\[6pt]
0 \le x \le h: & n(x) = \dfrac{f_1}{f_1+g_1}\left(1-e^{\left(\frac{x^2}{h^2}-1\right)\frac{\phi}{V_{\mathrm{norm}}}}\right) \\[6pt]
x > h: & n(x) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

These are the closed-form expressions used to generate the probability distribution in {ref}`Example 29.1 <sec_example_huxley>`.
