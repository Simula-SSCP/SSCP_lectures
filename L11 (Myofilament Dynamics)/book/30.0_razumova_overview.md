# State-based modeling and cooperativity

The Huxley model elegantly explains how crossbridges slide, but it assumes every single myosin head acts completely independently. In reality, the contractile proteins in a muscle cell interact heavily with one another. When one part of the system activates, it physically twists and pulls on neighboring proteins, making it easier for them to activate as well. This phenomenon is called **cooperativity**.

In this chapter, we will transition away from modeling the spatial probability distribution of crossbridges and move toward **state-based Markov modeling**. We will explore the Razumova (2000) model, which tracks the distinct physical states of a regulatory unit. We will unpack the mathematics of nearest-neighbor cooperativity, showing how proteins "help" each other, and you will implement these nonlinear interactions in a Python example to observe the resulting force development.
