# Module overview

In the previous modules, we traced the electrical action potential along the cell membrane and watched as it triggered a massive flood of calcium into the cytosol. But how exactly does this chemical cloud of calcium generate physical, mechanical force?

In this module, we dive into the microscopic machinery of the muscle cell: the **myofilaments**. We will trace the chronological history of computational muscle mechanics, from the earliest analytical formulations to modern, comprehensive contraction models.

:::{admonition} What you will learn
:class: tip

1. **The Huxley (1957) model:** The fundamental mathematics of the sliding filament theory and the derivation of the macroscopic Force-Velocity relationship.
2. **State-based modeling:** Transitioning from spatial probability distributions to discrete Markov state models of regulatory units.
3. **Cooperativity:** How neighboring crossbridges and regulatory proteins physically interact and "help" each other activate (the Razumova model).
4. **Comprehensive models:** Coupling calcium binding directly to crossbridge distortion to simulate a realistic, force-generating muscle twitch (the Rice model).
   :::

By the end of this module, you will implement these historical and modern models computationally, allowing you to mathematically bridge the gap between chemical binding and physical force.
