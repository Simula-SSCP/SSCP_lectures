# Modern comprehensive models

The Huxley (1957) model successfully explained the macroscopic sliding of filaments. The Razumova (2000) model successfully explained the complex state-based cooperativity of the regulatory units.

However, neither of these models explicitly simulated the actual chemical trigger for contraction: the calcium transient. In modern computational physiology, we need models that can link the electrical action potential directly to calcium release, and then seamlessly connect that calcium release to mechanical force production.

In this final chapter, we will explore the comprehensive muscle contraction model developed by **Rice, Wang, Bers, and de Tombe (2008)**. This is a highly robust "hybrid" model. It uses rigorous biophysics to map the cascade from calcium binding to crossbridge cycling, while intentionally simplifying the spatial mechanics to keep the computational cost low enough to be run simultaneously alongside a whole-cell action potential simulation.
