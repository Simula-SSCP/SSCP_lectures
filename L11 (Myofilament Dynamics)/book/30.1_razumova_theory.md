# The Razumova (2000) model

The stiffness-distortion model proposed by Razumova, Bukatina, and Campbell (2000) represents a half-sarcomere. Instead of tracking the exact spatial displacement $x$ of every crossbridge (XB) like Huxley did, this model groups the contractile machinery into distinct functional states.

The force generated by the sarcomere is simply the sum of the forces produced by all XBs that are currently in a force-generating state.

## The four states

The model tracks the proportion of regulatory units (RUs) in four specific states:

- $R_{\mathrm{off}}$: The thin filament is inactive. Myosin cannot bind.
- $D$: The detached state. The thin filament is active, but the XB has not attached yet.
- $A_1$: Attached XB, pre-powerstroke (generating no force).
- $A_2$: Attached XB, post-powerstroke (generating force).

By applying mass-action kinetics, we can define the ordinary differential equations (ODEs) governing the transitions between these states:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}D}{\mathrm{d}t} = k_{\mathrm{on}}R_{\mathrm{off}} + f'A_1 + gA_2 - (k_{\mathrm{off}} + f)D
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}A_1}{\mathrm{d}t} = fD + h'A_2 - (f' + h)A_1
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}A_2}{\mathrm{d}t} = hA_1 - (h' + g)A_2
$$

Because the total number of binding sites ($R_T$) must be conserved, we can always find the final state algebraically: $R_{\mathrm{off}} = R_T - D - A_1 - A_2$.

## Mathematical cooperativity

The true power of the Razumova model lies in how it handles **cooperativity**. The transition rates between states ($k_{\mathrm{on}}$, $f$, etc.) are _not_ constant. They dynamically change depending on what the neighboring proteins are doing!

The authors defined three distinct types of cooperative coupling:

### 1. RU-RU Cooperativity (Parameter $u$)

If a regulatory unit is adjacent to another RU that is already turned _on_, it requires less activation energy to turn on itself.

Let $\lambda^{\mathrm{on}}$ be the fraction of sites currently active ($\lambda^{\mathrm{on}} = \frac{D + A_1 + A_2}{R_T}$). The base activation rate $k_{\mathrm{on}}^u$ is scaled by a cooperativity parameter $u$:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}}^w = k_{\mathrm{on}}^u \left[1 + \lambda^{\mathrm{on}}(u - 1)\right]^2
$$

When $u = 1$, there is no cooperativity. When $u > 1$, the activation of a few sites triggers an avalanche of activation in their neighbors.

### 2. XB-XB Cooperativity (Parameter $v$)

Similarly, if a crossbridge attaches and undergoes a powerstroke ($A_2$), it physically deforms the filament, making it easier for neighboring detached crossbridges ($D$) to attach and enter the $A_1$ state.

Let $\lambda^{A_2}$ be the fraction of sites in the force-generating state. The attachment rate $f$ is scaled by a cooperativity parameter $v$:

$$
f = f_0 \left[1 + \lambda^{A_2}(e^{v-1} - 1)\right]^2
$$

### 3. XB-RU Cooperativity (Parameter $w$)

Finally, crossbridges and regulatory units interact with each other. If a crossbridge is strongly bound ($A_2$), it physically wedges the regulatory unit open, preventing it from turning off. The activation rate $k_{\mathrm{on}}$ is scaled further by a parameter $w$:

$$
k_{\mathrm{on}} = k_{\mathrm{on}}^w \left[1 + \lambda^{A_2}(e^{w-1} - 1)\right]^2
$$

By tuning these three cooperative parameters ($u$, $v$, and $w$), the Razumova model can perfectly reproduce the steep, sigmoidal force-pCa curves observed in real heart muscle tissue.
