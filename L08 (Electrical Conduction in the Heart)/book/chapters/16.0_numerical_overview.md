# Numerical methods and the bistable equation

In the previous chapter, we successfully derived the cable equation—a powerful partial differential equation (PDE) that completely describes how voltage changes across both time and space.

However, there is a catch: for complex biological systems, PDEs are famously difficult (and often completely impossible) to solve analytically with pen and paper. If we want to simulate how an action potential travels down an axon, we must use computers to approximate the solution numerically.

In this chapter, we will introduce **Finite Difference Methods (FDM)**, the foundational mathematical technique used to discretize continuous PDEs into algebraic equations that a computer can rapidly solve. We will then introduce the **bistable equation**, a simplified mathematical cousin of the Hodgkin-Huxley model. Finally, you will write your own FDM solver in Python to simulate a traveling wave of electricity propagating down a biological cable!
