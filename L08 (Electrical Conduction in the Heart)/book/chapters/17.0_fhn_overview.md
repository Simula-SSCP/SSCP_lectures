# The FitzHugh-Nagumo model and reentry

In the previous chapter, we successfully simulated a traveling wave of electrical activation using the bistable equation. However, if you watched the animation of your solution, you likely noticed a glaring biological inaccuracy: the wave acted like a localized forest fire, leaving the tissue permanently depolarized ($V=1$) in its wake.

Real action potentials do not stay depolarized forever. Thanks to delayed potassium currents, the membrane repolarizes back to its resting potential, making the tissue ready to fire again.

In this final chapter, we will upgrade our math to the **FitzHugh-Nagumo (FHN)** model, which introduces a "recovery" variable to pull the voltage back down to rest. We will then take our 1D biological cable and mathematically bend it into a continuous ring using **periodic boundary conditions**. Finally, you will use this ring to simulate a self-sustaining **reentry circuit**—the exact electrophysiological mechanism responsible for lethal cardiac arrhythmias!
