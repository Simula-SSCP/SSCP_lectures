# Conduction in excitable cells: The cable equation

In the previous modules, we treated the cell as a single, uniform point in space. We assumed that when an action potential fired, the entire cell membrane depolarized perfectly simultaneously. For small, compact cells, this is a reasonable simplification. However, biological cells—especially neurons and muscle fibers—can be incredibly long and complex structures.

If an action potential fires at one end of a meter-long motor neuron, how does that electrical signal physically travel down the axon to the other end?

In this module, we move from zero-dimensional models to one-dimensional space. We will explore the mathematical foundations of signal propagation, starting with the historical attempts to model 19th-century submarine telegraph cables. We will then derive the famous **cable equation** for biological tissue.

Finally, you will learn how to solve these complex spatial equations computationally using **Finite Difference Methods**, culminating in exercises where you will simulate traveling action potentials and dangerous electrical "reentry" circuits in the heart!
