# Scaling the cable equation

Before we solve the cable equation, it is mathematically useful to scale the variables to reduce the number of floating parameters and make it universally applicable.

Let us define a baseline membrane resistance $R_{\mathrm{m}}$:

$$
\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{m}}} = \frac{\Delta I_{\mathrm{ion}}}{\Delta V}(V_0)
$$

where $V_0$ is the resting potential. If we assume the axial resistances $r_i$ and $r_e$ are constant, we can multiply the entire cable equation by $R_{\mathrm{m}}$ to get:

$$
C_{\mathrm{m}} R_{\mathrm{m}} \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + R_{\mathrm{m}} I_{\mathrm{ion}} = \frac{R_{\mathrm{m}}}{p(r_i+r_e)} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
$$

We can group these physical constants into two incredibly important biological parameters:

- **The time constant ($\tau_{\mathrm{m}}$):** Defined as $\tau_{\mathrm{m}} = C_{\mathrm{m}} R_{\mathrm{m}}$. This dictates how fast the membrane voltage can change in time.
- **The space constant ($\lambda_{\mathrm{m}}$):** Defined as $\lambda_{\mathrm{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{R_{\mathrm{m}}}{p(r_i+r_e)}}$. This dictates how far a signal will physically travel down the cable before decaying.

By also defining the ionic current term as $f = -R_{\mathrm{m}} I_{\mathrm{ion}}$, we can rewrite the equation as:

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{m}} \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} - f = \lambda_{\mathrm{m}}^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2}
$$

Finally, we introduce strictly dimensionless variables for time ($T = t/\tau_{\mathrm{m}}$) and space ($X = x/\lambda_{\mathrm{m}}$). This gives us the beautifully clean, dimensionless cable equation:

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial T} = f + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial X^2}
$$

## The Reaction Term ($f$)

The form of the reaction term $f$ depends entirely on the specific biology of the cell we want to study!

- **For an excitable axon:** $I_{\mathrm{ion}}$ would be the complex, non-linear Hodgkin-Huxley equations we derived in the last module.
- **For a passive dendrite:** We use a simple linear resistance model ($I_{\mathrm{ion}} = \frac{V-V_0}{R_{\mathrm{m}}}$). In scaled form, this simply becomes $f = -V$, yielding the passive linear cable equation:

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial T} = \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial X^2} - V
$$

## Passive Conduction and the Space Constant

Consider a long, passive dendrite governed by the linear equation above. If we voltage-clamp the left end of the cable ($X = 0$) to a constant voltage $V_c$, the system will eventually reach a steady state where the voltage is no longer changing in time ($\frac{\partial V}{\partial T} = 0$).

The equation simplifies to:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial X^2} = V
$$

The mathematical solution to this simple spatial differential equation is an exponential decay:

$$
V(X) = V_c e^{-X} = V_c e^{-x/\lambda_{\mathrm{m}}}
$$

This tells us exactly how a signal decays along a passive cable! At a distance of exactly one space constant ($x = \lambda_{\mathrm{m}}$), the voltage will have decayed to $e^{-1}$, or roughly 37% of its original strength. At a distance of ten space constants, the signal will be almost completely erased.

This is exactly why large animals _must_ have active, regenerating action potentials (axons) to send signals over long distances, rather than relying purely on passive diffusion!
