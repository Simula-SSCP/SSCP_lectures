# The FitzHugh-Nagumo model

The bistable equation perfectly describes a propagating front, but because it only has one variable, it can only transition from 0 to 1. To describe a true, repeating action potential, we need to add a **recovery variable**.

The result is the FitzHugh-Nagumo (FHN) model. Developed in the early 1960s, it is a simplified 2D system that captures the qualitative essence of the Hodgkin-Huxley model without the crushing complexity of tracking four distinct gating variables.

The FHN model consists of two coupled partial differential equations:

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} = k \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + A V(1 - V)(V - \alpha) - w
$$

$$
\frac{\partial w}{\partial t} = \epsilon (V - \gamma w)
$$

Where:

- $V$ is the voltage (the fast activation variable).
- $w$ is the recovery variable (acting similarly to the outward potassium current and sodium inactivation combined).
- $\epsilon$ and $\gamma$ are small positive constants that dictate the speed and strength of the recovery.

## How the recovery variable works

To understand the dynamics, let's look at the temporal derivatives.

Initially, the tissue is at rest ($V=0$, $w=0$). If a stimulus pushes $V$ above the threshold $\alpha$, the cubic polynomial rapidly drives $V$ toward 1. This acts exactly like the bistable equation we just coded!

However, look at the second equation: as $V$ increases, $\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}$ becomes positive. Because $\epsilon$ is very small, $w$ grows _slowly_.

As the recovery variable $w$ finally gets larger, we look back at the first equation. The $-w$ term acts as a heavy anchor, eventually overpowering the cubic polynomial and pulling $\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}$ negative. This forces the voltage to crash back down toward 0 (repolarization).

Once $V$ drops back to 0, the second equation forces $w$ to slowly decay back to 0 as well, resetting the entire system for the next action potential. In a 1D cable, the solution to this system is a beautifully smooth, traveling action potential pulse!
