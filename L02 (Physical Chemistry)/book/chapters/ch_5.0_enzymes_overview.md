# Enzyme kinetics and the Michaelis-Menten equation

In this final chapter of the physical chemistry module, we will combine the core principles we have learned so far—the law of mass action, equilibrium, and fractional binding—to understand one of the most important classes of molecules in biology: **enzymes**.

Enzymes are the microscopic machines that make life possible. By speeding up chemical reactions that would otherwise take thousands of years to occur spontaneously, they act as the "on-switches" for cellular metabolism, signaling, and energy transfer.

We will first look conceptually at how enzymes speed up reactions by lowering activation energy barriers. Then, we will derive the famous **Michaelis-Menten equation**, a mathematical model that perfectly describes the reaction rates of most enzymes. Finally, we will preview how the very same mathematical framework we use for enzymes and chemical reactions will be applied to model the opening and closing of ion channels in the cell membrane in our upcoming lectures.
