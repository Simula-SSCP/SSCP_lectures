# Thermodynamics and transport

In our previous chapters, we have mathematically modeled how chemical reactions progress over time and how molecules bind together. However, we haven't yet discussed the fundamental physical forces driving these reactions in the first place.

Why do some reactions occur spontaneously while others require an input of energy? Why do molecules tend to spread out evenly across a space rather than bunching up?

In this chapter, we will ground our biophysical models in the strict physical constraints of the universe. First, we will explore the laws of **thermodynamics** and introduce **Gibbs free energy**, a powerful concept that combines energy and entropy to determine which cellular processes can spontaneously occur.

Then, we will look at **diffusion** and **Fick's law**, which mathematically describe the macroscopic transport of molecules driven by random thermal motion. These concepts are absolutely essential for understanding how and why ions flow across the cell membrane—a process we will spend significant time modeling later in this course.
