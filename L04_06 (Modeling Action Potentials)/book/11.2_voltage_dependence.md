# Voltage-dependent rates: Steady state and time constants

If the transition rates $\alpha$ and $\beta$ in our gating equation were strict constants, the open probability would simply settle to a single fixed value and stay there forever.

However, we know that the voltage sensor domains physically move in response to the electrical field. Therefore, the rate at which they transition must be strongly dependent on the membrane voltage $V$.

There are many mathematical ways to model this voltage dependence, but it is standard to use exponential relationships. For example, a simple formulation for an activation rate might look like this:

$$
\alpha(V) = A e^{k V}
$$

Alternatively, they can be written to highlight the voltage at which the rate equals 1 ($V_0$) and a slope factor ($d$):

$$
\alpha(V) = \exp\left(\frac{V - V_0}{d}\right)
$$

By defining $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as functions of voltage, our ODE for the gating variable $m$ (representing activation) becomes a dynamic, voltage-driven system:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}m}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha(V) (1 - m) - \beta(V) m
$$

## Reformulating the ODE: $m_\infty$ and $\tau_m$

While the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ formulation is how the equation is fundamentally derived from mass action, it is not very intuitive to look at. Electrophysiologists vastly prefer to rewrite this exact same ODE using two mathematically equivalent, but physically meaningful, variables: the steady-state probability ($m_\infty$) and the time constant ($\tau_m$).

Let's find the equilibrium (steady-state) solution for our gate. At equilibrium, the rate of change is zero ($\frac{\mathrm{d}m}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0$).

$$
0 = \alpha(1 - m) - \beta m
$$

Solving for $m$ gives us the steady-state value, which we call $m_\infty$:

$$
m_\infty(V) = \frac{\alpha(V)}{\alpha(V) + \beta(V)}
$$

Next, we define the time constant of the system, $\tau_m$, as the inverse of the sum of the two rates:

$$
\tau_m(V) = \frac{1}{\alpha(V) + \beta(V)}
$$

By doing a bit of algebraic substitution, we can perfectly rewrite our original mass action ODE using only these two new variables:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}m}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{m_\infty(V) - m}{\tau_m(V)}
$$

### Why is this formulation better?

This rewritten ODE tells an incredibly intuitive biological story!

- **The Target ($m_\infty$):** At any given voltage, the channel has a desired target open probability, $m_\infty$. If you hold the voltage constant, the gating variable $m$ will always drift directly toward this target.
- **The Speed ($\tau_m$):** The time constant tells you exactly _how fast_ the channel will reach that target. If $\tau_m$ is large, the channel opens very slowly (like $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$). If $\tau_m$ is small, it snaps open almost instantly (like $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$).

Because $m_\infty(V)$ represents a transition between two distinct energy states (closed and open) driven by the electrical field, its shape almost always perfectly follows a thermodynamic **Boltzmann equation** (an S-shaped sigmoidal curve).
