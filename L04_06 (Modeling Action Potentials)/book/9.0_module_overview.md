# Introduction to action potential models

In the previous module, we established that the cell membrane acts as a capacitor, and that opening ion channels allows electrodiffusive currents to flow, shifting the membrane potential. We assumed that these channels simply "opened" for a fixed duration to produce a spike.

In reality, the generation of an action potential is a highly dynamic, beautifully orchestrated event. Ion channels are complex molecular machines that physically alter their shape—opening and closing—in response to the membrane potential itself. This process is called **ion channel gating**.

In this module, we will explore how models of excitable cells are built from the ground up, catering to both computational neuroscience and cardiac electrophysiology. We will start by examining the physical structure of ion channels and the historical experiments—famously performed on the squid giant axon—that revealed their function.

Next, we will mathematically formalize this behavior by deriving the **Hodgkin-Huxley** equations. Rather than relying on black-box simulations, you will write the functions for these gating variables yourself and assemble them to fire your very first simulated action potential. Finally, we will look at how modern researchers use advanced Markov models to simulate the dazzling variety of specific neural and cardiac currents found in the human body.
