# Module overview

In the previous module, we established that the cell membrane acts as a capacitor, and that opening ion channels allows electrodiffusive currents to flow, shifting the membrane potential. We assumed that these channels simply "opened" for a fixed duration to produce a spike.

In reality, the generation of an action potential is a highly dynamic, beautifully orchestrated event. Ion channels are complex molecular machines that physically alter their shape—opening and closing—in response to the membrane potential itself. This process is called **ion channel gating**.

In this module, we will explore how models of excitable cells are built from the ground up, catering to both computational neuroscience and cardiac electrophysiology.

:::{admonition} What you will learn
:class: tip

1. **Ion channel structure and history:** The physical properties of ion channels and the classic voltage-clamp experiments performed on the squid giant axon.
2. **The Hodgkin-Huxley equations:** How to mathematically formalize voltage-sensitive gating using differential equations.
3. **Action potential simulation:** Writing the code to combine individual gating variables and fire your very first simulated action potential.
4. **Modern cardiac and neural models:** How researchers use advanced Markov models to capture the dazzling variety of specific currents found in the human body.
   :::

By the end of this module, you will have moved past black-box simulations, giving you the tools to mathematically assemble and code a complete excitable cell model from scratch.
