# Modeling molecular transitions with mass action

To build our model, we start with the Ohmic equation for an ion current passing through a population of channels of type $\mathrm{X}$:

$$
I_{\mathrm{X}} = g_{\mathrm{X}} (V - E_{\mathrm{X}})
$$

The term $g_{\mathrm{X}}$ represents the total conductance of all the channels of this type in a specific patch of membrane. We can express this macroscopic conductance explicitly in terms of the underlying channels:

$$
g_{\mathrm{X}} = N_{\mathrm{X}} \cdot G_{\mathrm{X}} \cdot P_o
$$

Where $N_{\mathrm{X}}$ is the total number of channels, $G_{\mathrm{X}}$ is the microscopic conductance of a _single_ open channel, and $P_o$ is the **open probability** (the fraction of channels that are currently open).

Because $N_{\mathrm{X}}$ and $G_{\mathrm{X}}$ are physical constants for a given cell, they are typically combined into a single maximum conductance parameter, denoted with a bar: $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{X}} = N_{\mathrm{X}} G_{\mathrm{X}}$.

Our current equation becomes:

$$
I_{\mathrm{X}} = \bar{g}_{\mathrm{X}} \cdot P_o \cdot (V - E_{\mathrm{X}})
$$

It is only $P_o$ that represents the dynamic, time-varying function of the channel itself. To model $P_o$, we must rely on the law of mass action.

## Deriving the gating equation

Let us assume that channel activation occurs due to a single molecular transition between a Closed ($\mathrm{C}$) state and an Open ($\mathrm{O}$) state. We define the forward rate constant as $\alpha$ and the backward rate constant as $\beta$:

$$
\mathrm{C} \underset{\beta}{\overset{\alpha}{\rightleftharpoons}} \mathrm{O}
$$

Applying the standard law of mass action, we can define the rate of change of the open state:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}[\mathrm{O}]}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha [\mathrm{C}] - \beta [\mathrm{O}]
$$

Because this conventional mass action expression provides $\mathrm{C}$ and $\mathrm{O}$ as raw concentrations, we can divide by the total number of channels ($[\mathrm{C}]+[\mathrm{O}]$) to convert them into probabilities. If we call the probability of being open $n$, then the probability of being closed must be $(1 - n)$.

Substituting these probabilities into our mass action equation gives us the fundamental differential equation for a gating variable:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha (1 - n) - \beta n
$$

## Independent sub-units

For some channels, transitioning from closed to open is a single, simple step. However, recall from the previous chapter that tetrameric potassium channels have _four_ identical voltage sensors, all of which must shift outward before the pore can physically open.

If we assume that each of these four transitions is identical and unaffected by the others (i.e., they are statistically independent), then the total open probability $P_o$ will simply be the product of the probability of all four subunits being in the permissive state.

Therefore, for the classic Hodgkin-Huxley potassium current, the open probability is:

$$
P_o = n^4
$$

Where $n$ represents the probability of a _single_ voltage sensor being activated, governed by the mass action ODE we just derived. When we plug this back into our Ohmic current equation, we get the famous formulation:

$$
I_{\mathrm{K}} = \bar{g}_{\mathrm{K}} n^4 (V - E_{\mathrm{K}})
$$
