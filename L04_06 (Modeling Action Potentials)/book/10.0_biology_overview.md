# The biological and experimental basis of gating

Before we can build mathematical equations for ion channel currents, we need to understand the physical reality of what we are modeling.

In this chapter, we will explore the structure-function relationships of voltage-gated ion channels. We will look at how a channel physically "senses" electricity, and the mechanisms by which it opens and closes.

We will then review a brief history of the experimental techniques that made computational electrophysiology possible—from the two-electrode voltage clamps used on squid axons to the modern patch-clamp technique. We will conclude with a computational exercise demonstrating how the chaotic, stochastic flickering of single ion channels perfectly averages out to create the smooth macroscopic currents we observe in whole cells.
