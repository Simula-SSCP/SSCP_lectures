# The Hodgkin-Huxley gating framework

In the previous chapter, we established the biological reality of ion channels. We know that they are physical proteins with voltage sensor domains (VSDs) that shift in response to the electrical field, causing the pore to open (activate) or close (deactivate). We also know that when thousands of these stochastic channels act together, they create a smooth, predictable macroscopic current.

But how do we translate this biological understanding into a rigorous mathematical model?

In this chapter, we will walk through the exact mathematical framework developed by Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley in 1952. We will apply the laws of chemical mass action to derive the gating equations, introduce voltage-dependent rate constants, and define the crucial concepts of the **steady-state probability** ($m_\infty$) and the **time constant** ($\tau_m$). Finally, we will review the specific experimental protocols researchers use to measure these parameters in a lab.
