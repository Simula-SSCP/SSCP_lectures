# Modern cardiac and neural models

The Hodgkin-Huxley model of the squid giant axon is one of the most successful mathematical models in the history of biology. However, mammalian cells—such as human cardiomyocytes and cortical neurons—are vastly more complex than the squid axon.

A human ventricular heart cell, for example, does not just have two currents. It has dozens of distinct ion channels, pumps, and exchangers, all interacting simultaneously. Furthermore, the cardiac action potential lasts for hundreds of milliseconds, featuring a prolonged "plateau" phase that is critical for allowing the heart to physically contract. Mammalian neurons also possess a dazzling variety of specialized currents that allow them to burst, adapt, and integrate signals.

In this final chapter, we will explore the "zoo" of modern ion currents, discussing the specific roles of sodium, calcium, and various potassium channels. We will then discuss why the classic Hodgkin-Huxley equations often fail to capture complex drug interactions, necessitating the use of advanced **Markov models**. Finally, you will get hands-on experience modifying the conductances of a comprehensive human ventricular cell model to see how changing a single current can drastically alter the shape of the action potential.
