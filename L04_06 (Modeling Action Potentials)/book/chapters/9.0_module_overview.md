# Module overview

In the previous module, we established that the cell membrane acts as a capacitor, and that opening ion channels allows electrodiffusive currents to flow, shifting the membrane potential. We derived the Nernst potential and used it to define Ohmic currents. We even built a very simple action potential model by manually toggling conductances on and off.

In reality, ion channels are not simple on/off switches. They are complex molecular machines that physically change shape in response to the membrane potential—opening and closing on their own, with precise voltage sensitivity and timing. This process is called **ion channel gating**, and it is the key ingredient that transforms a passive membrane into an excitable cell capable of firing action potentials.

In this module, we will explore how models of excitable cells are built from the ground up. We will start from the biology and experimental techniques that first revealed gating, develop the mathematical framework of Hodgkin and Huxley, and progressively build a computational model that produces a realistic action potential.

:::{admonition} What you will learn
:class: tip

1. **Ion channel structure and experiments:** The physical properties of voltage-gated ion channels and the classic experiments that revealed how they work.
2. **The Hodgkin-Huxley gating equations:** How to formalize voltage-sensitive gating using differential equations derived from the law of mass action.
3. **Action potential simulation:** Progressively building from simple conductance models to a complete excitable cell model, culminating in your first simulated action potential.
4. **Modern cardiac and neural models:** How researchers extend the HH framework with specialized currents and advanced Markov models to capture the complexity of mammalian cells.
:::

The material covered in this module is relevant to both computational neuroscience and cardiac electrophysiology. The Hodgkin-Huxley framework we develop here remains the foundation of virtually every modern cell model in use today.
