# The biological and experimental basis of gating

In the previous module, we derived that the membrane potential changes according to:

```{math}
:label: eq:membrane_ode_recap
\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{m}}} \sum_k g_k (V - E_k)
```

We treated the conductances $g_k$ as fixed parameters. But if these conductances were truly fixed, the membrane potential would simply relax to a weighted average of the Nernst potentials—there would be no action potential, no excitability, nothing interesting at all.

The action potential exists because ion channel conductances are _not_ fixed. They change dynamically, driven by the membrane potential itself. This creates a powerful feedback loop: the voltage controls the channels, which control the currents, which control the voltage. Understanding how this feedback works requires understanding the physical reality of ion channels.

In this chapter, we will examine the structure of voltage-gated ion channels, the molecular mechanisms by which they open and close, and the groundbreaking experiments that first allowed scientists to observe these processes directly. By the end of this chapter, you will have a solid biological foundation for the mathematical models we develop next.
