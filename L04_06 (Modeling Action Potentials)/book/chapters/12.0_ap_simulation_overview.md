# Simulating the action potential

We now have all the mathematical ingredients needed to simulate an action potential from scratch. We know how to write Ohmic currents ($I = g(V - E)$), and we know how to model the voltage-dependent gating variables ($m$, $h$, $n$) that dynamically control the conductances.

What remains is to put everything together: to assemble the full Hodgkin-Huxley equivalent circuit, write the coupled system of ODEs, and solve them numerically.

The result will be something remarkable. From just four differential equations—one for the membrane potential and three for the gating variables—and a handful of parameters fitted to squid axon data, the model will spontaneously produce a realistic action potential. It will exhibit a sharp threshold, an all-or-nothing response, a refractory period, and even repetitive firing under sustained stimulation. All of these emergent properties arise naturally from the interplay between the fast sodium activation, the slower sodium inactivation, and the delayed potassium activation.

In the exercises accompanying this chapter, we will build up to the full model progressively: starting with a simple two-current model with fixed conductances, then adding a single voltage-dependent gate, and finally assembling the complete Hodgkin-Huxley system. This step-by-step approach will help you understand _why_ each component is necessary and what it contributes to the action potential.
