# Assembling the full cell model

We now have all the pieces needed to build a complete model of an excitable cell. We have the Ohmic equation for ionic currents, the gating ODEs for the activation and inactivation variables, and the specific rate functions fitted by Hodgkin and Huxley. The final step is to put everything together into a single coupled system of differential equations.

## The equivalent circuit

The starting point is the equivalent circuit of the Hodgkin-Huxley model. Each ionic current is represented as a variable conductance (a resistor whose value changes over time) in series with a battery (the Nernst potential for that ion). The membrane capacitance sits in parallel with all the ionic conductances.

According to Kirchhoff's current law, the capacitive current must perfectly balance the sum of all ionic currents and any externally applied stimulus current:

$$
C_{\mathrm{m}} \frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = - (I_{\mathrm{Na}} + I_{\mathrm{K}} + I_{\mathrm{L}}) + I_{\mathrm{app}}
$$

## The Hodgkin-Huxley currents

The classic Hodgkin-Huxley model of the squid giant axon contains three distinct ionic currents:

**1. The sodium current ($I_{\mathrm{Na}}$).** This is the fast inward current responsible for the upstroke of the action potential. The sodium channel has both an activation gate ($m$) and an inactivation gate ($h$). Because the channel structure requires three of the four domains to activate independently, the $m$ variable is cubed:

$$
I_{\mathrm{Na}} = \bar{g}_{\mathrm{Na}} \, m^3 \, h \, (V - E_{\mathrm{Na}})
$$

**2. The delayed rectifier potassium current ($I_{\mathrm{K}}$).** This is the outward current responsible for repolarization. It has an activation gate ($n$) but no inactivation gate—once opened, it stays open as long as the membrane is depolarized. It has four identical voltage sensors, so the $n$ variable is raised to the fourth power:

$$
I_{\mathrm{K}} = \bar{g}_{\mathrm{K}} \, n^4 \, (V - E_{\mathrm{K}})
$$

**3. The leak current ($I_{\mathrm{L}}$).** This represents the constant background leak of ions (primarily chloride) through non-gated channels. It is not voltage-dependent, so it has no gating variables:

$$
I_{\mathrm{L}} = \bar{g}_{\mathrm{L}} \, (V - E_{\mathrm{L}})
$$

## The complete system of ODEs

Putting everything together, the full Hodgkin-Huxley model is a system of **four coupled ordinary differential equations**: one for the membrane potential and one for each gating variable.

$$
C_{\mathrm{m}} \frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\bar{g}_{\mathrm{Na}} m^3 h (V - E_{\mathrm{Na}}) - \bar{g}_{\mathrm{K}} n^4 (V - E_{\mathrm{K}}) - \bar{g}_{\mathrm{L}} (V - E_{\mathrm{L}}) + I_{\mathrm{app}}
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}m}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha_m(V)(1-m) - \beta_m(V) m
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha_h(V)(1-h) - \beta_h(V) h
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha_n(V)(1-n) - \beta_n(V) n
$$

Notice the coupling: the voltage equation depends on the gating variables (through the currents), and the gating equations depend on the voltage (through the rate functions). This mutual dependence is what creates the complex, nonlinear dynamics of the action potential.

## The "tug-of-war" of the action potential

When we solve these coupled equations, the generation of an action potential emerges as a beautifully choreographed sequence of events—a thermodynamic tug-of-war between competing ionic currents:

1. **Stimulus and threshold.** An applied current $I_{\mathrm{app}}$ or synaptic input slightly depolarizes the membrane. If the depolarization reaches a critical threshold (around $-55$ mV), it triggers the next step.

2. **Depolarization (Na⁺ activation).** The fast-acting $m$ gates of the sodium channels snap open within a fraction of a millisecond. Sodium rushes _into_ the cell, driven by both the concentration gradient and the electrical gradient. This inward current depolarizes the membrane further, which opens more $m$ gates, which lets in more sodium—a runaway positive feedback loop that drives $V$ rapidly toward $E_{\mathrm{Na}} = +50$ mV.

3. **Repolarization (Na⁺ inactivation + K⁺ activation).** Two slower mechanisms terminate the upstroke. First, the $h$ gates of the sodium channels begin to close (inactivation), cutting off the inward sodium current. Second, the $n$ gates of the potassium channels finally open, allowing potassium to rush _out_ of the cell, forcefully pulling the membrane potential back down toward $E_{\mathrm{K}} = -77$ mV.

4. **Afterhyperpolarization.** The potassium channels remain open briefly even after the voltage has returned to rest, because $n$ decays slowly. This excess outward potassium current pushes the membrane potential _below_ the resting potential temporarily, before the $n$ gates finally close and the membrane returns to its resting state.

## Standard parameters

The table below summarizes the classic Hodgkin-Huxley parameter values for the squid giant axon at 6.3 °C. These are the values you will use in the exercises.

| Parameter               | Symbol                  | Value | Unit   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- | ----- | ------ |
| Membrane capacitance    | $C_{\mathrm{m}}$        | 1.0   | µF/cm² |
| Max. Na⁺ conductance    | $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{Na}}$ | 120   | mS/cm² |
| Max. K⁺ conductance     | $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{K}}$  | 36    | mS/cm² |
| Leak conductance        | $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{L}}$  | 0.3   | mS/cm² |
| Na⁺ reversal potential  | $E_{\mathrm{Na}}$       | +50   | mV     |
| K⁺ reversal potential   | $E_{\mathrm{K}}$        | -77   | mV     |
| Leak reversal potential | $E_{\mathrm{L}}$        | -54.4 | mV     |
