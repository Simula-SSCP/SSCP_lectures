# Modern cardiac and neural models

The Hodgkin-Huxley model of the squid giant axon is one of the most successful mathematical models in the history of biology. However, mammalian cells—such as human cardiomyocytes and cortical neurons—are vastly more complex than the squid axon.

A human ventricular cardiomyocyte does not just have two ionic currents. It has dozens of distinct ion channels, pumps, and exchangers, all interacting simultaneously. The cardiac action potential lasts for hundreds of milliseconds—roughly a hundred times longer than the squid axon action potential—featuring a prolonged "plateau" phase that is critical for allowing the heart to physically contract. Mammalian neurons also possess a dazzling variety of specialized currents that allow them to burst, adapt, and integrate signals in ways the squid axon cannot.

In this chapter, we will explore the "zoo" of modern ion currents, discussing the specific roles of sodium, calcium, and various potassium channels in shaping different types of action potentials. We will then discuss why the classic HH gating formalism sometimes falls short, and how **Markov models** provide a more flexible framework for capturing complex channel behavior—particularly important for modeling drug-channel interactions. Finally, you will get hands-on experience modifying the conductances of a comprehensive human ventricular cell model.
