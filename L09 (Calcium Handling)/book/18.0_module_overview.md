# Module overview

In the previous modules, we focused entirely on the outer boundary of the cell. We studied the action potential, the voltage-gated ion channels that generate it, and the equations governing spatial electrical conduction.

Now, we shift our focus inward. We will explore what happens _inside_ the cytosol following an action potential, focusing primarily on intracellular **calcium handling**. Calcium is the crucial physiological messenger that couples the electrical activation of the cell to its physical contraction—a beautifully orchestrated process known as **excitation-contraction (EC) coupling**.

:::{admonition} What you will learn
:class: tip

1. **The calcium transient:** How L-type calcium channels and Ryanodine Receptors (RyRs) trigger massive, explosive releases of intracellular calcium.
2. **Calcium cycling:** The delicate thermodynamic balance between the influx of calcium and the efflux driven by the SERCA pump and the Sodium-Calcium Exchanger (NCX).
3. **Pump and exchanger modeling:** Moving from simple Michaelis-Menten approximations to complex, multi-state Markov models of transport proteins.
4. **Autoregulation:** How the cell dynamically regulates these pathways in response to adrenaline or drugs like caffeine.
   :::

In the accompanying exercises, you will write Python code to simulate these complex calcium cycling dynamics in both simplified neurons and highly detailed human cardiomyocytes.
