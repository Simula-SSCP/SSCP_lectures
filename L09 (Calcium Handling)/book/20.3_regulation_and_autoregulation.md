# Regulation and autoregulation

The body has several mechanisms to dynamically regulate EC coupling and change the force of a heartbeat on the fly.

## Beta-adrenergic stimulus (Adrenaline)

When you exercise or get scared, adrenaline binds to $\beta$-receptors on the outside of the cardiomyocyte. This triggers a signaling cascade that activates **Protein Kinase A (PKA)** and **CaMKII**.

These kinases aggressively phosphorylate targets across the cell:

1. They upregulate L-type calcium channels (letting more trigger calcium in).
2. They downregulate **Phospholamban (PLB)**. PLB normally acts as the "brakes" on the SERCA pump. By removing the brakes, SERCA runs much faster!

As SERCA speeds up, the SR load dramatically increases. A higher SR load means the RyRs release a much stronger calcium transient on the next beat, leading to a massive increase in contractile force. This is why a healthy heart exhibits a **positive force-frequency relationship**—the faster it beats, the harder it squeezes.

## Autoregulation (The caffeine experiment)

Cardiomyocytes are also inherently self-regulating. Consider a famous experiment where a cell is paced at a regular rate, and then a dose of **caffeine** is added.

Caffeine binds to the RyRs, increasing their sensitivity and making them open much wider.

```{figure} ../fig/autoregulation0.png
---
width: 500px
name: fig_autoregulation
---
Caffeine artificially boosts the calcium transient, causing a strong contraction. But over the next 10 beats, the cell slowly autoregulates back down to baseline. *(Adapted from Sjaastad et al., 2003)*
```

**Why does the cell return to baseline?**
When the caffeine forces the RyRs to release an abnormally huge burst of calcium, the cytosol floods. The NCX exchanger suddenly sees a ton of calcium and works overtime, pumping a large portion of it _out_ of the cell entirely.

Because this calcium left the cell, SERCA has less calcium available to pump back into the SR. On the next beat, the SR load is lower. This lower SR load cancels out the sensitizing effect of the caffeine! Within a few beats, the cell reaches a new steady-state equilibrium where the transient is the exact same size as before, just operating at a lower overall SR load.
