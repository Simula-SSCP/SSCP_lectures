# Excitation-contraction coupling

Over 200 years ago, Luigi Galvani famously reported that the leg muscles of dead frogs would twitch if he applied an electrical spark. This was the birth of the study of **excitation-contraction (EC) coupling**, the process where electrical activation causes a muscle to physically contract.

The action potential occurs exclusively at the cell membrane, but actual physical contraction occurs deep within the cell in organelles called myofilaments. For excitation and contraction to be coupled, a signal must travel from the depolarizing membrane into the cytosol. It turns out that this signal is mediated by a massive, temporary influx of diffusing calcium ions ($\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$).

In this chapter, we will look at how calcium acts as a signaling molecule, map the internal structures of the cardiomyocyte that handle it, and walk step-by-step through the creation and decay of the **calcium transient**.
