# Calcium cycling and balance

The process outlined above repeats for every single beat of the heart. Calcium is _cycled_ back and forth between different domains.

Because all calcium entering the cytosol mixes together, the two efflux proteins—**NCX** and **SERCA**—effectively _compete_ for the same calcium to clear it.

- If SERCA wins the tug-of-war, more calcium is stored in the SR (increasing the **SR load**). A higher SR load means the RyRs will release a much larger transient on the _next_ beat.
- If NCX wins the tug-of-war, calcium leaves the cell entirely, decreasing the SR load.

Because the heart beats continuously, it cannot increase or decrease its SR load indefinitely. The system will spontaneously regulate toward a steady-state balance where the amount of calcium released from the SR per beat equals the amount pumped back in by SERCA:

```{math}
:label: eq:calcium_balance
\int J_{\mathrm{RyR}} \, \mathrm{d}t = \int J_{\mathrm{SERCA}} \, \mathrm{d}t
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
\int J_{\mathrm{LTCC}} \, \mathrm{d}t = \int J_{\mathrm{NCX}} \, \mathrm{d}t
```

## Species differences

The balance between internal and external calcium cycling differs drastically across mammalian species and substantially alters the cell's behavior.

In species with extremely fast heart rates and short action potentials (such as rats and mice), the LTCC channels do not remain open for long. To compensate, these cells rely heavily on massive internal SR calcium release and consequently possess very strong SERCA pumps relative to their NCX.

Conversely, larger mammals with slower heart rates (such as humans and rabbits) have long action potential plateaus. Their LTCC channels stay open for an extended period, so they are less reliant on SR stores. In these species, NCX plays a much larger role.

## Disease

The balance between internal and external calcium cycling is not only different between species, but also between different cell types, and it can change with maturation and disease. **Heart failure**, for instance, is frequently associated with a severe downregulation of SERCA function. As SERCA weakens, NCX clears progressively more calcium out of the cell, leading to a decrease in SR load and resulting in weak, failing contractions.
