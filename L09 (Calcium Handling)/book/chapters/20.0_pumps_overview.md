# Pumps, exchangers, and regulation

During the action potential, calcium floods into the cytosol, triggering the myofilaments to contract. However, for the heart to beat again, it must first relax. This requires clearing the calcium out of the cytosol against its massive concentration gradient.

In this chapter, we will look closely at the mathematical models of the two main actors responsible for this calcium efflux: the **SERCA pump** (which refills the sarcoplasmic reticulum) and the **Sodium-Calcium Exchanger (NCX)** (which ejects calcium out of the cell).

We will explore how these models scale in complexity—from simple Michaelis-Menten equations to heavy, multi-state Markov models. Finally, we will examine how the cell dynamically regulates these pumps in response to adrenaline (epinephrine) and how it beautifully auto-regulates its own calcium balance when perturbed by drugs like caffeine.
